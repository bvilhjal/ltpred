# Independent review of ltpred v0.3.4 (2026-08)

!!! warning "Historical document"

    This review audits **v0.3.4** at commit `cf8cc99`. It predates the 0.4.0
    lean-down, so some machinery it checks (for example
    `scripts/check_results.py`) no longer exists, and its findings have since
    been addressed or superseded. Kept for provenance; do not read it as a
    description of the current release.

A read-only, peer-review-style audit of the statistical core, the estimation and
fitting APIs, the supporting numerics, the test and CI machinery, the benchmark
evidence base, the documentation, and the literature positioning. This review
covers the tree at commit `cf8cc99` (v0.3.4); it follows, and is independent of,
the archived v0.3.0 review in `EXTERNAL_REVIEW.md`. No files were modified by
the review.

## 1. Scope and method

The review proceeded in three stages. First, the project's own gates were run:
the full test suite, the linter, the evidence guards, and a strict documentation
build (Table 1). Second, seven areas were reviewed in parallel against the
primary sources: the code, `docs/algorithm.md`, the benchmark artifacts in
`benchmarks/`, and the published literature. Third, every finding reported here
at tiers T1 and T2 was re-verified independently against the code or the
artifacts — each number quoted below was recomputed from the committed CSVs or
reproduced by execution. Severity tiers: **T1**, correctness or scientific
validity; **T2**, robustness, evidence integrity, or maintainability; **T3**,
style, clarity, or polish.

## 2. Verdicts by area

**Statistical core (`covariance.py`, `thresholds.py`, `pearson_aitken.py`,
`gibbs.py`).** Correct. The bound conventions (case/control orientation,
onset pins, interval censoring), the Pearson–Aitken rank-1 update, the
censoring-mixture weights, the precision-form Gibbs full conditionals, and the
new collapsed sweep were verified against `docs/algorithm.md` eqs. (1)–(11),
against closed-form truncated-normal conditionals, and against
`scipy.stats.truncnorm`/high-precision references, including the far-tail
regimes (|z| ≈ 38.5 handoff) and near-degenerate intervals. Kinship
coefficients, the tabular method (including inbreeding), and the PD correction
were checked on hand-built pedigrees. Cross-engine agreement on probe families
matches within Monte-Carlo error.

**Estimation and fitting (`estimate.py`, `fit.py`, `simulate.py`).** Sound.
The `LiabilityResult` separation of Monte-Carlo SE (`se`, shrinks with draws)
from posterior variance (`var`, does not) is the statistically right
bookkeeping and is documented candidly. The contract gates (multi-trait vs
`c2`/`m2`, Gibbs vs mixture) behave as documented with no silently-accepting
path found. The IPW construction is a correct Horvitz–Thompson weighting over
families, and the case-rate screen, the Haseman–Elston-type fixed-point
fitter, and the family-cluster bootstrap all match their documentation. The
three onset models in `simulate.py` were verified numerically against their
specification.

**Supporting numerics (`cip.py`, `tetrachoric.py`, `liability_scale.py`,
`_mathfun.py`, `_numba.py`, `pedigree.py`, `family.py`).** Correct where it
matters. Kaplan–Meier and Aalen–Johansen estimates *and* their Greenwood/Aalen
standard errors match R `survival`/`cmprsk` to all printed digits under ties,
delayed entry, and risk-set exhaustion; the tetrachoric MLE matches `polycor`
to ~1e-5; the Lee et al. liability-scale transforms round-trip exactly; AS 241
matches `scipy.special.ndtri` to 6e-16 relative error down to p = 1e-300; the
Numba JIT kernels are bit-identical to the pure-Python fallback.

**Tests and CI.** The oracle quality is high: closed forms, locked R outputs,
and machine-precision kernel checks, with justified tolerances and disciplined
seeding. The guard machinery genuinely recomputes from artifacts (no
circularity found). Gaps are in CI coverage and in one vacuous research test
(F2), plus the absence of a build/packaging leg and of an R-lock regression
fixture (F15, F16).

**Evidence base.** Strong and honest in the main. All load-bearing guarded
claims recompute exactly from the committed artifacts (83/83 guards), nearly
every headline number carries the right kind of uncertainty (Monte-Carlo SE vs
sampling variability is separated explicitly and repeatedly), and the
interpretation is restrained — the λ_GC = 16.46 result is framed as a
deliberate confounding demonstration, NCP claims carry their independent-SNP
marginal-association simulation regime (not real-LD mixed-model evidence), and
speedups carry their thread count. The defects found are one stale
superlative (F1), a few last-digit/ordering transcription slips in unguarded
prose (F7, F8, F17), and provenance gaps concentrated in the pre-schema-3
history (F9, F10).

**Documentation.** Accurate against the code: every documented parameter name
and default checked matched; the quickstart executes verbatim; the equations
and Algorithms G/P/M match the implementations step for step. Two numerical
claims in the docs lack artifacts (F11, F12).

**Literature positioning.** Fair and accurate. Every load-bearing citation was
verified against the publisher record — Pedersen et al. 2022 (AJHG 109:417–432,
LT-FH++), Pedersen et al. 2023 (Nat Commun 14:5553, ADuLT), Dybdahl Krebs et
al. 2024 (AJHG 111:2494–2509, PA-FGRS), Hujoel et al. 2020 (Nat Genet
52:541–547, LT-FH), Hujoel et al. 2022 (Cell Genomics 2:100152), and the
forward-looking Dybdahl Krebs et al. 2026 (AJHG 113:184–201, DOI
10.1016/j.ajhg.2025.11.016 — confirmed real; the 2025 in the DOI is the epub
year). No misattribution: PA is consistently credited to Krebs et al. 2024,
and the repo even reports Hujoel 2020's *unfavourable* PA comparison rather
than cherry-picking. `CITATION.cff` is correct entry by entry. The defects are
cosmetic (F33–F35).

Two interpretation remarks. First, the effect sizes are reported with the
*right* uncertainty almost everywhere — Monte-Carlo SEs for simulation means,
paired intervals for few-replicate contrasts — and the document repeatedly
warns against substituting one for the other (§5's SD/MC-SE ratio discussion is
exemplary). Second, the claims most at risk of over-reading are precisely the
ones this review flags: a stale superlative (F1) and unguarded transcriptions
(F7, F8, F17). The guard system works; these fell through its gaps.

## 5. Limitations of this review

1. No benchmark was re-run end to end; claims were verified by recomputation
   from the committed artifacts and provenance files. The R locks in
   particular were not re-executed (F16 covers the implication). This is a
   structural blind spot, not merely an economy: recomputation establishes
   that a number follows from its artifact, and is silent on whether the
   *experiment* behind the artifact was like-for-like. The fold-time row of
   Table 5 is the worked example — 6.87 ± 0.12× re-derives exactly and was
   still not the implementation comparison its label promised, because
   ltpred ran on four threads and the R packages on one worker. Comparability
   has to be audited by re-running under matched conditions, which is what
   `bench_ltfhplus_compare_1thread.csv` now does.
2. The `research/` package was reviewed at the level of its tests and public
   contracts, not by a full theoretical audit of its EM/MCEM derivations.
3. Literature verification used the publisher records accessible on
   2026-08-16; one sub-clause (F35) could not be confirmed from accessible
   text and is flagged rather than asserted false.
4. Numerical probes were run on one machine and one stack (Table 1); nothing
   here speaks to Windows or to NumPy/SciPy floors beyond what CI covers.

## 6. Summary

The statistical core is correct, the numerics are verified against independent
references, the test oracles are sound, the evidence discipline is real, and
the literature framing is fair. The two T1 findings are narrow: one stale
