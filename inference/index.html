    Fitting model parameters¶

    Fit liability-scale heritability, genetic/residual correlations and shared-environment covariance from family data. If those parameters are already available, go to Scoring. Unsupported prototypes stay in research extensions.

    Supported sampling contract

    All four fitters assume independent, non-overlapping families and common case/control thresholds. When pid is supplied, a person appearing in more than one family raises. Use estimate_liability for overlapping register pedigrees.

    Use sampling="population" for an unascertained sample. For selection on observed status, use sampling="ipw" with one reciprocal known, strictly positive inclusion probability per family. Omitting sampling warns and assumes population sampling. No weight can reconstruct a stratum with zero inclusion probability, and bootstrap_fit does not repair selection bias.

    Thresholds assert a prevalence, so each role's case rate is screened for gross inconsistency. A mismatch can mean ascertainment or the wrong prevalence. The screen is not a certificate: at K = 0.05 its detectable enrichment is about 1.67× at N = 1,500 and 1.26× at N = 10,000, with a 1.15× floor binding at large N. The benchmark shows 1.51× enrichment already inflates h² by +0.48.

    The meaning of se differs by method. Haseman–Elston (HE) fits report a within-dataset Monte-Carlo diagnostic, not sampling uncertainty. Pairwise fits report conditional, asymptotic family-cluster sampling SEs for identifiable interior estimates. bootstrap_fit provides approximate family-cluster uncertainty when its design assumptions hold.

    Choose a fitter¶

    Table 1. Fitters for independent families with common binary thresholds.

    fitter use when computation and uncertainty
    fit_heritability additive h² only stochastic moment fixed point; family bootstrap
    fit_variance_components separate A, C and/or M with identifying relationships stochastic moment fixed point; family bootstrap
    fit_pairwise deterministic single-trait A/C/M composite likelihood; interior cluster-sandwich SEs
    fit_pairwise_multi joint trait h², genetic/residual correlations, optional C/M multivariate composite likelihood; interior cluster/delta-method SEs

    Personalised CIP/onset bounds are for scoring, not these fits. Identification requires jointly observed relationship contrasts. The stochastic fitters are one to two orders of magnitude slower at their defaults; shortening their Gibbs schedules requires separate Monte Carlo checks. See the benchmark ledger for measured recovery and cost.

    Fitting heritability from the family data¶

    fit_heritability alternates Gibbs augmentation of latent liabilities with a damped Haseman–Elston update. This is a stochastic-approximation fixed point, not posterior sampling: the trace is not a posterior chain and its mean is not a posterior mean.

    from ltpred import fit_heritability
fit = fit_heritability(fitting_families, sampling="population")
fit.h2, fit.h2_se       # estimate and within-fit MC diagnostic

    Relatives are required. fit.h2_se is not an inferential SE and understated across-dataset variability in benchmarks; use bootstrap_fit for that. The fitter requires one common threshold per trait and rejects personalised bounds, onset pins and two-sided intervals. That is the validated implementation scope, not evidence that heterogeneous thresholds invalidate the moment identity. estimate_liability still accepts personalised bounds because it conditions on h2 rather than fitting it.

    Family-cluster uncertainty: bootstrap_fit¶

    from ltpred import bootstrap_fit
bs = bootstrap_fit(
    families,
    lambda f: fit_heritability(f, seed=1, sampling="population").h2,
    n_boot=100,
)
bs.estimate, bs.se, (bs.ci_low, bs.ci_high)

    The callback can return any scalar or array from a fitter. Fix its internal seed so spread reflects family resampling rather than sampler noise. For IPW, pass weights=weights and use a two-argument callback such as lambda f, w: fit_heritability(f, sampling="ipw", weights=w, seed=1).h2; families and weights are resampled by the same indices.

    Cost is n_boot + 1 fits. The interval assumes independent clusters and a valid sampling design; zero-probability strata, overlapping pedigrees and boundary parameters need separate treatment. Increase n_boot until the SE and percentile endpoints are stable and report the value used.

    Deterministic pairwise fitting¶

    fit_pairwise estimates the same A/C/M fractions from binary relative-pair probabilities without sampling latent liabilities:

    from ltpred import fit_pairwise
pw = fit_pairwise(fitting_families, components=("A", "C"), sampling="population")
pw.components, pw.residual
pw.se, pw.inference_status

    Components need independent identifying contrasts among observed pairs. Fractions are nonnegative and sum to at most 1 - eps (1e-6 by default). Failed, infeasible or nonstationary optimization raises. IPW weights are normalised to mean one; the weighted marginal screen does not prove joint positivity or weight correctness.

    For an interior fit, pw.covariance follows pw.component_order and pw.se is its diagonal SD. The sandwich uses family-level score variation, so pairs sharing a member are not treated as independent. It is conditional on supplied thresholds and weights and is asymptotic, not a finite-sample calibration claim. At a component/residual boundary (within max(1e-7, 10*tol)), at_boundary is true and covariance/SEs are NaN; inference_status also distinguishes insufficient clusters or information. Ordinary normal intervals and likelihood-ratio tests are then inappropriate. Bootstrap/profile inference at a boundary needs its own calibration.

    Joint heritability and cross-trait correlations¶

    from ltpred import fit_pairwise_multi
joint = fit_pairwise_multi(
    two_trait_families, components=("A", "C", "M"), sampling="population")
joint.h2, joint.rg, joint.re
joint.se["h2"], joint.se["rg"], joint.se["re"]
joint.components["C"], joint.correlations["C"]
joint.inference_status

    Each member needs one bound per trait. Thresholds may differ between traits but must be common across people within each trait. Missing phenotypes use (-inf, inf); see the input recipe.

    With phenotype-major coordinates, the fitted model is

    \[ \Sigma = G\otimes A + S\otimes C + T\otimes M + E\otimes I, \qquad \operatorname{diag}(G+S+T+E)=1. \tag{1} \]

    components=("A",) omits S and T. Every component is PSD and residual E has eigenvalues at least eps. C means full-sibship environment; M describes spousal resemblance, not the inheritance consequences of assortative mating.

    h2 = diag(G), rg standardises G and re standardises residual E. Shared-environment correlations are separate in correlations["C"] and correlations["M"]. rp sums all components, so rp - genetic_cov is total non-genetic covariance and differs from env_cov when C/M is present. These are model decompositions, not causal effects.

    Only jointly observed coordinates contribute. Missingness must preserve pair distributions; imputing a completely missing relative creates no identifying contrast. Family-cluster sandwich SEs and the correlation delta method are conditional on thresholds and weights. parameter_order indexes free covariance entries and parameter_covariance their joint uncertainty. A boundary in any component withholds all normal SEs; the tolerance is max(1e-7, 10*sqrt(tol)). Correlations at or below that variance are NaN.

    benchmarks/bench_pairwise_multi.py covers both correlation signs and nulls, A+C+M, MCAR, IPW and omitted shared environment. Its evidence is limited to the tested designs. Multi-trait scoring is A+E, so do not pass a C/M fit's rp as full_corrmat: that would incorrectly treat shared covariance as residual E.

    Ascertained samples¶

    The HE dose-response (bench_ascertainment.py, nuclear families, true h² = 0.5, K = 0.05, N = 2,000, two replicates) shows why selection matters:

    realised case share ÷ assumed K 0.95× 1.51× 1.96× ≥ 2×
    fitted h² 0.420 0.984 0.998 1.000

    This is bias, not just noise: the proband-ascertained arm remains at +0.500 bias from N = 1,000 to 10,000 and across initial values. See RESULTS §29.

    When you can correct it: sampling="ipw"¶

    For a known design with positive inclusion probability π in every stratum, pass weights = 1 / π:

    import numpy as np
from ltpred import fit_heritability

# 50/50 case/control cohort from population prevalence K = 0.05:
keep_p = 0.05 * 0.5 / (0.5 * 0.95)
weights = np.where(proband_is_case, 1.0, 1.0 / keep_p)
fit = fit_heritability(families, sampling="ipw", weights=weights)

    Augmentation within a given family is already conditional on its statuses; selection breaks the mix of families, which the weights restore. In the benchmark this returns case-enriched fits from the boundary to near the true 0.5.

    Two limits remain:

    • Positivity. No finite weight reconstructs an unobserved stratum. Ascertainment through an affected proband is the standard failure. Weighted marginal case rates can falsify bad weights but cannot establish joint positivity.
    • Efficiency. At K = 0.05 a 50/50 cohort needs weights up to 19×, greatly reducing effective sample size. IPW buys accuracy with precision.

    ltpred cannot infer weights from the data. Family-history selection is correctable only when every complete observed family pattern has a known, strictly positive inclusion probability.

    A scale correction is not a selection model

    A Lee observed→liability factor depends only on (K, P) and cannot repair these fits: at fixed K, true h² = 0 and 0.5 can both return 1.000, so no invertible constant maps both back. The fitters already estimate on the liability scale. Use observed_to_liability_h2 only to convert an observed-scale estimate from another method.

    Sensitivity to the assumed heritability¶

    import numpy as np
from ltpred import estimate_liability
grid = [0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7]
scores = [estimate_liability(families, h2=h).est["genetic"] for h in grid]
min_corr = np.corrcoef(scores)[np.triu_indices(len(grid), 1)].min()

    In the calibration benchmark, assumed h² 0.2–0.8 barely changed ranking (corr with true g 0.429–0.431) while the calibration slope moved from 2.24 to 0.68: it acted mostly as a near-linear rescaling in that design, not as proof of rank invariance. Probe prevalence/CIP sensitivity by rebuilding the bounds and comparing scores. Low cross-setting correlation is a reason to seek a better external estimate or, only under the sampling contract, fit one.

    Variance components (A / C / M)¶

    from ltpred import fit_variance_components
vc = fit_variance_components(families, ("A", "C"), sampling="population")
vc.components["A"], vc.components["C"], vc.residual

    C is identified only by full-sib pairs. vc.se has the same Monte-Carlo-only meaning as h2_se; use bootstrap_fit for sampling uncertainty. With ("A",) the result matches fit_heritability.

    M loads on genetically unrelated mate pairs (parents and grandparent couples). It describes spousal resemblance from shared adult environment and/or assortative mating but is not a generative assortative-mating model. Because mates have A = 0, omitting M usually biases A much less than omitting C. Every identifying pair set must be present. Dominance is unsupported because the role bank provides no independent dominance kernel. C and M kernels are PSD equivalence-class partitions with unit diagonal; residual variance is 1 - sum(components), floored at eps.

    Unsupported research prototypes¶

    HE multi-trait genetic-correlation and onset-age-decay fitting, common-factor and genetic-nurture models, MCEM and parametric-bootstrap tests live in research/advanced_fitting.py. They are available only from a source checkout and are not part of the supported distribution. See research extensions.
